01

Strategy

Learning and engagement are best approached as doing by example.

When building a curriculum, I treat it the way I'd treat a product roadmap. I start with a foundational understanding of the subject matter, then embed the learning through case studies and short workshops. I usually work alongside other instructors and TAs to build the roadmap for the whole team.

Curriculum as roadmap

  • Foundation first — establish the subject matter before asking anyone to apply it.
  • Embed through case studies and short workshops rather than lecture alone.
  • Built collaboratively with other instructors and TAs, not handed down.

02

Discovery

Teaching is research driven — and my knowledge is limited to a specific place in time when it was relevant to me.

Technology moves fast, especially in my field of work, so I allocate time to update what I know in order to keep presentations relevant. I also research ongoing world events I might tie an exercise to, so students can practise problem-solving around current issues rather than hypothetical ones.

Keeping material current

  • Knowledge has a shelf life — dedicated time set aside to refresh material before each engagement.
  • Exercises anchored to real, current events so problem-solving has real stakes.
  • Ethics of design thinking as a recurring throughline across audiences and age groups.
03

Approach

I'm mindful of the time allotted for each class, and I build the agenda from there — covering the five Ws, then how to apply the subject matter taught.

In my experience, 10–15 minutes of speaking keeps an audience engaged; any longer and students start to absorb less. So I break classes with an exercise after every 10–15 minutes, putting what I just covered into practice to help with understanding and memorization. Those activities run 5–20 minutes depending on the task. I always allocate time for sharing and feedback, leave 10 minutes after speaking for follow-up questions, and 15–20 for an audience I've never engaged with before.

Here is a sample class I built and taught on Car UX/UI as an industry partner at University of Toronto iSchool's Master's of UXD program.

Session structure

  • Agenda built backwards from the time allotted, covering the five Ws first.
  • Never more than 10–15 minutes of speaking before an exercise.
  • Sharing and feedback built into the schedule, not squeezed in at the end.
  • Longer Q&A buffer for audiences I haven't taught before.
