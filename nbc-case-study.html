The NBC Network was going through a transformation to build an identity — which later became Peacock TV — that captures all NBC brands under one umbrella, creating a one-stop shop for discoverability across all NBC content.

01

Challenge

The NBC Network contains different channels that cater content to various personas.

The client wanted a TV experience encompassing existing and future channels that could scale, letting users surf all content without downloading channel-specific apps. Working closely with the client's design team, we designed an experience aligned with NBC's vision to keep brands distinct while offering one seamless discoverability layer.

Why it was hard

  • Multiple channels serve different personas and needed distinct identities inside one shared app.
  • The structure had to scale for channels and content that didn't exist yet.
  • No channel-specific downloads — everything had to live in a single discoverability layer.

02

Strategy

I met with the design team at 30 Rockefeller Plaza in New York to whiteboard concepts.

At this stage, we needed to decide on the sizing of image assets so the NBC design team could add them to their backend for our development team to load via the API. Once assets were determined, I started wireframing the experience for functionality sign-off from stakeholders.

Where decisions were made

  • On-site whiteboarding with NBC's design team, in person, at 30 Rock.
  • Image asset sizing was locked early so NBC's backend and the dev team's API stayed in sync.
  • Wireframes were built specifically for stakeholder sign-off on functionality, not just visual review.
Fig. 02 Recreated crop of the on-site whiteboard session — aspect ratios and shelf types (Today's Highlights, Continue Watching, Channels) were locked here before any high-fidelity design began.
03

Approach

With an understanding of the product's UX, we worked closely with the NBC design team to translate their new brand identity into the 10-foot experience.

We adjusted colors to be broadcast-safe and avoid eye strain, along with contrast and sizing adjustments for visual accessibility. The result was a working prototype with a remote to mimic key assignments. The NBC team then took this concept to internal stakeholders — which sent it back to the drawing board for a new concept that is now Peacock TV.

Ship considerations

  • Broadcast-safe color adjustments to avoid eye strain on TV displays.
  • Contrast and sizing adjustments for 10-foot visual accessibility.
  • A working prototype with a physical remote mapped to real key assignments.
