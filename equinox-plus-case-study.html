The Equinox team was searching for a design partner to bring their digital app to TV. Equinox+ gives members unlimited at-home access to every Equinox brand — and Renga shaped the 10-foot experience strategy, designing for Roku, Android TV, Fire TV and Apple TV.

01

Challenge

Translating the Equinox+ mobile experience into a cohesive TV system — across four platforms that don't behave the same way.

Each of Roku, Android TV, Fire TV and Apple TV brings its own navigation model, control scheme and technical envelope. The system needed one universal information architecture with UX flows that could diverge where a platform demanded it, without fragmenting into four separate products.

Why it was hard

  • Four platforms, four sets of platform requirements, one brand experience to hold together.
  • A cinematic workout experience depends on rich imagery — and imagery is exactly what the tightest platform couldn't afford.
  • Mobile-to-TV isn't a port: content density, input model and viewing distance all change at once.

The single biggest constraint on the project: Roku enforces a package file-size limit, and a cinematic workout browse experience is made almost entirely of imagery. Every asset decision in the system was made against this ceiling.

Fig. 01 Reconstruction of the core tradeoff. Designing to the most constrained platform first — rather than designing richly and cutting back — is what kept one system viable across all four.
02

Strategy

A universal IA, with independent UX flows where platform requirements demanded them.

The architecture stays constant — the same content model, the same hierarchy, the same mental map for a member moving between devices. What flexes underneath is navigation behaviour, asset weight and the interaction patterns each platform's remote actually supports.

My role on this was client-facing: coordinating requirements with the Equinox team, and teaching Renga's designers how a 10-foot experience differs from everything they'd built before.

What stayed fixed, what flexed

  • Fixed — information architecture, content model, brand expression, core browse hierarchy.
  • Flexed — navigation patterns, focus and remote behaviour, image weight and caching strategy.
  • Decided per platform — anything the platform's own guidelines mandated.

03

Discovery

We prototyped early so engineering could argue with us.

Design prototypes went to the engineering team specifically so they could raise feasibility concerns against real technical requirements — before the system hardened. In parallel, the client used the prototypes to compare the TV experience against their mobile app and flag features that hadn't made the translation.

What prototyping surfaced

  • Technical feasibility concerns raised by engineering while changes were still cheap.
  • Feature gaps between the mobile app and the TV build, identified by the client directly.
  • Platform-specific edge cases that only appear once a flow is walked with a real remote.
04

Outcome

An MVP TV experience shipped across four platforms, and a design system handed off to Equinox's in-house team to carry forward.

Design QA held the MVP in line with brand guidelines and the mobile experience. Honestly: the shipped MVP continued to have technical issues in calendar and filtering functionality — a reminder that a design system can be sound and still land on an engineering reality that isn't finished yet.

The outcome I care most about is the third tile. A client engagement that ends with your own team more capable than it started is worth more than the deliverable.
