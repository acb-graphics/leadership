AT&T released a DirecTV app for mobile and wanted to extend their offerings into the connected wearables and TV space — letting users stream breaking news, live sports and premium entertainment, anytime, anywhere.

01

Challenge

The focus was to release a Wear OS companion watch app that syncs seamlessly with the customer's DirecTV set-top box and mobile app.

The IoT experience had to feel like one continuous system — where a user could complete an action on one device and transition to another without having to redo it, in order to keep watching.

Why it was hard

  • Three connected surfaces — set-top box, mobile app, watch — had to stay in sync in near real time.
  • An action started on one device couldn't force the user to repeat steps on another.
  • Wear OS's screen size and input model limited how much of the TV-remote metaphor could translate to the wrist.
02

Strategy

With the team, we whiteboarded user flows from one device to another and bucketed our thinking into two scenarios: at home and out of home.

The at-home experience extends the set-top box's remote to the user's phone and watch — reading a synopsis of what's live and recording it to a specific set-top box in the home, e.g. "Record Westworld in the Living Room." The out-of-home experience notifies the user when a favorite show goes live, with the ability to record it to a connected set-top box at home for viewing later.

Two scenarios mapped

  • At home — remote-control parity: browse, play, and record straight from the wrist.
  • Out of home — live-show notifications with remote recording to a chosen set-top box.
  • Edge cases — no receiver paired, receiver offline, multiple receivers in one household.

03

Discovery

This was a tech-heavy discovery.

I worked closely with developers to understand the possible contingency messaging based on how the devices communicated with each other. It was an iterative process to understand the technical and design limitations of Wear OS. A demo was built from the wireframes and shown to stakeholders at AT&T for sign-off.

Discovery focus

  • Contingency messaging — what the watch shows when a device is offline, unpaired, or mid-buffer.
  • Wear OS constraints — screen size, input methods, and platform limits shaped what could realistically ship.
  • A working demo, not static wireframes, was what got the room to sign off.
Fig. 04 Reconstruction of the demo used with AT&T stakeholders — showing the same title playing in sync across the living-room TV and the connected watch.
04

Approach

We had to work closely with AT&T's copywriters throughout this project too.

We created a high-fidelity mockup guide illustrating UI and motion design, labeled the mockups where copy still needed to be provided, and worked with the writing team to make sure the brand's voice and tone stayed aligned across every connected device.

What shipped

  • A high-fidelity mockup guide covering UI and motion design.
  • Screens labeled for copy handoff, keeping tone consistent across devices.
  • A released Wear OS app connecting the set-top box and mobile app into one continuous experience.
05

Outcome

The end result was a release of the Wear OS app that connects with the user's set-top box and mobile app — bringing live TV browsing, playback and recording to the wrist as one synced system.
