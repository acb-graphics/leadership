Case Study — 0→1 Product Strategy & Research

Home

From founder idea to tested product strategy, brand positioning, and hiring roadmap — in 8 weeks.

Client
Home Financial Technologies Inc.
Role
Lead — Research, Design, Concept Testing
Duration
8 weeks
Product
joinhome.com

A founder had a vision: combine savings and advice in one product to help first-time homebuyers reach a down payment. What the founder didn't have was validation.

01

Overview

I led research, concept testing, and prototyping alongside a visual designer and strategist from Renga.

The eight-week engagement spanned discovery through final prototype. Each research decision was leveraged: survey respondents fed interviews, interviews shaped concepts, concepts were tested against survey segments.

Scope and methods

  • Discovery phase. 32-person survey of first-time homebuyers, follow-up interviews, competitive landscape review, founder interviews.
  • Methods. Qualitative and quantitative research, design workshops, concept testing, interactive prototyping.
  • Tools. Mix of Figma for prototype and design, spreadsheets and docs for research synthesis and flow documentation.
02

Problem

First-time homebuyers face two separate problems with two separate solutions. No product addresses both.

The market has financial products (savings apps, mortgage calculators) and advice products (blogs, podcasts, broker consultations). Survey respondents were using multiple tools but expressed exhaustion with the cognitive load of stitching them together. Anxiety about the buying process itself was constant across segments — even high-income buyers feared making the wrong decision.

Why it hadn't been solved

  • Ambiguous path forward. Existing solutions addressed financial optimization or emotional reassurance, but not both in a way that felt integrated.
  • No trusted source. Buyers were skeptical of anyone selling them a financial product, and equally skeptical of unsourced advice from strangers online.
  • Anxiety and doubt. The buying process itself felt risky and irreversible. Buyers needed assurance they were making the right move.
03

Research

I fielded a 32-person survey of first-time homebuyers, then recruited follow-up interviews from respondents. The survey segmented buyers by financial stage (saving, ready, financing) and explored their emotional and practical needs in each phase.

The insight that shifted the project's direction: buyers wanted both financial optimization and emotional validation, but they weren't looking for these as separate features. They wanted a single voice they could trust across both domains. That meant positioning was as important as product design.

What the research revealed

  • Dual need, single voice. Buyers wanted financial tools and emotional support, but treating them as separate features fragmented the experience. They needed one trusted guide.
  • Segment patterns held across demographics. Financial stage mattered more than age or income. A buyer in the "saving" stage had different anxiety than one "ready to close."
  • Competitive positioning was available. No existing product combined both dimensions. The market gap was real and defensible.
04

Process

  1. 01

    Survey and segment interviews. I designed and fielded a 32-person survey, then recruited follow-up interviews from respondents. This compressed discovery and ensured qualitative findings were grounded in a broader sample rather than anecdotal.

  2. 02

    Competitive and positioning workshops. Mapped four competitors on financial sophistication and emotional positioning axes, then workshopped Home's differentiation with the founder and strategy team. Positioning shaped everything downstream.

  3. 03

    Concept testing and iteration. Built three messaging approaches and tested each against the survey segments. Refined positioning based on which framing resonated most. Prototyped the core interaction (personalized question flow) and validated it with respondents.

  4. 04

    Waitlist landing page and investor readiness. Designed and launched a waitlist page to test messaging before any product existed. Created final prototype and documentation the founder could use to brief the incoming team and fundraise.

05

Solution

Home combines savings tools and buying advice under one positioning: a friend on the path to homeownership.

The core interaction is a personalized question flow that sequences content based on where buyers are financially and emotionally. The positioning met buyers' actual anxieties head-on rather than minimizing them — a differentiation from competitors focused purely on optimization or reassurance.

What was delivered

  • A validated problem statement grounded in primary research instead of founder intuition.
  • Positioning differentiated enough to carry an investor conversation and guide hiring.
  • A tested prototype demonstrating the core experience end to end.
  • A product roadmap and messaging strategy the client could execute independently.
06

Impact

The founder went from needing an agency to prove the idea, to needing a team to build it.

Home launched on the direction defined in these eight weeks: combined savings and advice, opened by a personalized question flow. The branding and research informed the investor deck. The live product is at joinhome.com.

Outcomes

  • Founder hired a permanent team to execute the strategy.
  • Strategy and positioning shaped product roadmap and investor messaging.

What worked

We prioritized research over assumptions. Letting real user needs reshape the project's direction made the recommendation credible. Using survey respondents for interviews streamlined concept testing — we could validate findings against a known segment rather than chasing new voices.

What I'd do differently

I'd pair network-sourced survey respondents with a screened panel. Fast and free is real at eight weeks, but not defensible. I'd also instrument the waitlist page from day one — it generated signal but I can only describe it, not quantify it.

What I learned

In a 0→1 engagement, the deliverable isn't the prototype. It's the client's ability to continue without you. This project's measure of success was that the founder could hire a team, write job descriptions, and defend the direction in a room I wasn't in.
