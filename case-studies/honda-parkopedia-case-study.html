Honda × Parkopedia

Reserving a parking space is three different products depending on whether the car is moving, approaching, or stopped.

Client
Honda R&D Innovations
Product
In-vehicle parking search and payment
Role
Lead Product Designer
Duration
About 3 months, 2017

Honda R&D Innovations wanted to build a proof of concept for finding and paying for parking inside the vehicle, built on their auto platforms with Parkopedia.

01

Overview

A proof-of-concept engagement: flow architecture, in-vehicle interface design, and an interactive prototype.

Nothing here shipped. The outcome I'd defend is the reframing. The work turned a partial happy-path concept into a complete definition of the problem.

Team, tools & scope

  • Team. I was the sole designer from my side, paired with one Honda in-house designer, working with TribalScale engineers and consulting Parkopedia's technical team on integration questions.
  • Tools. Sketch for flows and mockups; InVision for the prototype.
  • Methods. Design audit, driving-state modeling, technical discovery, competitive review, prototyping.
  • Scope. 3 driving states · payment-security approach established · error states across both lanes.
02

Problem

Honda's in-house team had already produced designs — but they covered the version where everything goes right, and only for the approaching-the-destination state. In a car, almost nothing is that simple.

Honda R&D Innovations was evaluating whether in-vehicle parking payment was a viable direction and whether Parkopedia was the right partner. A payment transaction inside a moving vehicle is a safety question before it's a UX question, and parking decisions arrive under time pressure when attention is least available.

These pain points were reasoned from the driving context, not confirmed with drivers.

Why it had stalled

  • Only the happy path existed. Error states weren't designed, which in a vehicle means no response when it matters.
  • The in-motion state hadn't been considered. A fundamentally different problem, and it was absent.
  • Payment security was an unknown across two platforms. Nobody had established what CarPlay or Android Auto would permit.
03

Research

No user research was run. Discovery was a design audit, a competitive review, a review of automotive app design principles, and a technical investigation.

I analyzed Honda's existing work against the full set of user states, which is how the two gaps surfaced. A competitive review of automotive systems and standalone parking apps showed how much of the category assumed a stationary user with a phone in hand. I also experienced the journey myself, noting the full path from being in the car to finding parking.

The concept was reviewed with stakeholders and the TribalScale engineers — design review, not user validation.

Key findings

  • Driving state, not user type, is the organizing variable. The same task splits into three products depending on whether the driver is moving, approaching, or stopped.
  • The happy path was the smallest part of the problem. Most of the real experience lives in the states the original designs didn't cover.
Fig. 01 The two driving states added to Honda's original happy path — one for the in-motion, voice-driven flow, one for the parked, touch-driven flow.
04

Process

  1. 01

    Audited the existing designs and named what wasn't there. Mapping the full set of user states turned this from execution into definition.

  2. 02

    Split the experience into three driving states: moving, approaching the parking spot, and stopped. Honda's happy path covered approaching; filling in moving and stopped was the work. Voice-driven confirmation while moving, touch-driven search and payment when stopped.

  3. 03

    Drove the payment-security discovery. Established that payment had to be handled by Parkopedia's system before it could break the design.

  4. 04

    Built an interactive prototype showing what was possible. TribalScale engineers then used it to build a proof of concept for Honda in Android Auto.

05

Solution

A complete in-vehicle parking concept covering three driving states, with a payment flow that runs through Parkopedia's system.

In motion, the interface keeps major actions prominent, uses short sentences, and narrates aloud what it is asking, while offering nearby lots and taking voice confirmation — no map manipulation, no payment entry. Parked, it opens up to map search, price and ETA comparison, and touch-confirmed payment. Approaching the parking spot, the third state, started from Honda's original happy-path design. The product is the complete journey: driving, deciding where to park as you approach, and parking.

The screen was reduced to what makes the decision — price, ETA, and a garage/street toggle. Everything else was cut: a driver reading it is a driver not watching the road. Error states were designed rather than deferred.

TribalScale engineers used the prototype to build a proof of concept for Honda in Android Auto — a proof of concept whose value was definition, not shipment.

Fig. 02 In-vehicle infotainment mockup — price, ETA to destination, and a garage/street toggle, with reserve and navigate as the two primary actions.
06

Impact and results

Honda leadership engaged TribalScale to explore the art of the possible for in-vehicle parking payment, and TribalScale engineers used the prototype to build a proof of concept for Honda.

The engagement resolved the payment-security question the proof of concept depended on, and established the in-motion and error-state design that hadn't existed. Concept work has no usage metrics.

What this did not do

  • This concept did not ship, and makes no claim on any Honda parking feature released since. Parkopedia announced in-vehicle parking payment for Honda in mainland China in October 2021, through a different partner (MXNavi); that work is separate from this engagement.

What worked

Refusing to design forward from an incomplete map. Auditing the in-house designs and naming what was missing reframed the engagement from execution to definition. Pulling the payment-security discovery forward mattered too — finding out late would have invalidated the flows.

What I'd do differently

Get the in-motion flow in front of drivers, even crudely — a static rig or simulator would have tested whether voice confirmation and short narrated prompts are genuinely glanceable or just look that way on a desk.

What I learned

Context is a design constraint with more force than any brand or platform requirement. Designing for the worst context is what made this concept survive contact with engineering.
