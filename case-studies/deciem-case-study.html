Case Study — Cross-Functional Research & Experience Strategy

Deciem

Persona building, product strategy and omnichannel experimentation

Client
Deciem | The Abnormal Beauty Company (The Ordinary, NIOD, Hylamide)
Role
Senior UX Consultant
Started
2019-2010?

Deciem had a clear internal understanding of its product strategy and recognized an opportunity to deepen its insight into how customers viewed its brands and the company overall.

01

Project overview

Deciem hired me as its first Lead Researcher and UX Strategist, giving me responsibility for research and strategy from the outset.

I led all research activities, defined the strategy, owned participant recruitment, and created the feature prioritization framework. I also directed the initial experience design and prototyping.

Team, tools & scope

  • Team. Led research, strategy, and prototyping.
  • Tools. Google Analytics, Spark AR Studio, Figma.
  • Methods. 1:1 interviews, card sorting, analytics-led usability scripting, AR prototyping.
  • Scope. 5 departments · 3 brands · 2 AR concepts.
02

Problem statement

Customer service teams regularly interacted with customer feedback, while marketing and design teams had less direct exposure to these insights.

Deciem expanded quickly across brands and focused on product education and sustainability. Teams tackled different problems without a unified vision.

Customers made 94% of contacts to request product guidance they couldn't find themselves, and teams typically shared this information with those less involved in decision-making.

Challenges

  • The team's strategy was guided by a strong conviction about growth, with evolving access to structured customer insight.
  • Each department developed its own perspective, and teams less directly connected to customers often contributed significantly to strategic decisions.
  • We built research infrastructure and alignment at the same time, so the process and outcome moved together toward the same goal.
03

Research and discovery

Two tracks ran at once: internal interviews surfaced where the five departments disagreed, while external research focused on screens analytics flagged as failing.

We held 1:1 sessions with each department. Customer sessions were split into 30 minutes for Q&A on shopping and sustainability, and 30 minutes for usability testing. A handful of participants aged 24–64 took part.

The regimen guide drew over a million page views with a 46% bounce rate. This was included in the usability script and confirmed by a second signal: 94% of happiness-team contact was guidance customers could not find themselves.

Key findings

  • The key finding was an influence gap. Teams shaping the brand had no exposure to customer service feedback.
  • Regimen pages challenged non-expert users, who sometimes struggled to understand the reasoning behind Deciem's recommended combinations.
  • Sustainability interest outpaced infrastructure. Deciem ran zero-waste boxes in stores but had nothing online to support that.

04

Design process

  1. 01

    I interviewed all five departments separately to ensure all perspectives were represented and to identify differences constructively.

  2. 02

    I ran card sorting to force consensus. Each pain point became a card, and teams ranked them live.

  3. 03

    I used analytics to decide what to test, then wrote the highest-friction screens into the usability script.

  4. 04

    Tested three regimen-page variants. By skin type, by ingredient function, by routine.

  5. 05

    I prototyped two Spark AR concepts: Virtual Foundation Matching with the happiness team, and a Terracycle filter extending the in-store zero-waste program online.

Decisions and rationale

Unfiltered interview footage, not a summarised deck, shaped the outcome most because it removed the room's ability to argue with the interpretation.

What got in the way

Without formal authority or an established research practice, I used card sorting to enable teams to collaboratively build the priority list. When priorities differed — for example, marketing emphasized campaign visibility while customer service concentrated on product clarity — I facilitated open discussion to ensure all viewpoints were acknowledged and aligned with customer feedback. By focusing on genuine customer needs and collaboratively sorting priorities, the process fostered shared ownership of decisions. The key takeaway was that influence emerged through collective participation.

Fig. 02 Usability testing on the three regimen-page variants.
05

Solution

A two-tier prioritization roadmap separated immediate fixes from a larger scope of work, with two Spark AR concepts aimed at Deciem's two live strategic questions.

Virtual Foundation Matching let users see a shade on their own face, calibrated by the team handling the products daily. The Terracycle filter identified a bottle and routed it to the correct recycling stream. Spark AR was the strategic choice because customers already used it. The regimen-page testing produced a component library the internal team kept using.

What was delivered

  • A shared, cross-departmental understanding of positioning and customer need, which clarified the alignment outcome directly.
  • A roadmap separating quick wins from larger scope-of-work.
  • Two AR concepts addressing education and sustainability beyond retail.

06

Impact and results

Five departments aligned on one prioritized view of customer need; a two-tier roadmap gave leadership a clear sequencing decision.

The personalized regimen builder shipped on theordinary.com as a direct result of the research insights and roadmap I delivered; I owned the prioritization and feature requirements that guided its development. The Spark AR concept launched publicly. Deciem's 2020 goals for the regimen guide — conversion up to 5%, bounce down to 30% — were met. While I cannot directly attribute these metrics to my work alone, the roadmap and research I provided were key inputs as the team worked toward those targets.

What shipped, what didn't

  • The personalized regimen builder launched — the clearest outcome of the research.
  • Both AR concepts stayed at concept stage. The main reasons were a shift in business priorities toward core product improvements and resource allocation to other initiatives. Additionally, technical constraints took longer than planned to meet Deciem's quality standards. This experience reinforced the importance of adapting to changing business needs and identifying early on where to focus efforts for the greatest impact.

What worked

Making the research undeniable, not just persuasive. Card sorting let teams build the priority list themselves; unfiltered footage removed the room's ability to argue with the interpretation.

What I'd do differently

Negotiate a clear attribution model before starting rather than relying on company-wide analytics after the fact. Widen the sample beyond Toronto. For future projects, I would prioritize setting up attribution models in collaboration with stakeholders from day one to ensure impact is measurable and recognized across teams. I would also expand recruitment to include more geographically and demographically diverse participants, tailoring the sample to match the context and business goals. By embedding these lessons early in a new project, I can drive clearer alignment and demonstrate outcomes more effectively.

What I learned

The gap in most organizations isn't a lack of customer insight — it is that the insight sits with the teams that have the least power to act on it.
