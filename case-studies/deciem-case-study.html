Case Study — Cross-Functional Research & Experience Strategy

Deciem

Persona building, product strategy and omnichannel experimentation.

Client
Deciem | The Abnormal Beauty Company
Role
Senior UX Consultant
Duration
2019-2020
Product
The Ordinary

Deciem had a clear internal understanding of its product strategy and recognized an opportunity to deepen its insight into how customers viewed its brands and the company overall.

01

Overview

Deciem hired me as its first Lead Researcher and UX Strategist, giving me responsibility for research and strategy from the outset.

I led all research activities, defined the strategy, owned participant recruitment, and created the feature prioritization framework. I also directed the initial experience design and prototyping.

Team, tools & scope

  • Scope. 5 departments · User testing facilitation across 3 brands · 2 Spark AR concepts · 1 shipped regimen builder on theordinary.com.
  • Tools. Google Analytics, Spark AR Studio, Figma, Adobe Creative Suite.
  • Methods. 1:1 departmental interviews, card sorting, analytics-led usability scripting, AR concept prototyping.
02

Problem

Customer service teams regularly interacted with customer feedback, while marketing and design teams had less direct exposure to these insights.

Deciem expanded quickly across brands and focused on product education and sustainability. Teams tackled different problems without a unified vision.

Customers made 94% of contacts to request product guidance they couldn't find themselves, and teams typically shared this information with those less involved in decision-making.

Challenges

  • The team's strategy was guided by a strong conviction about growth, with evolving access to structured customer insight.
  • Each department developed its own perspective, and teams less directly connected to customers often contributed significantly to strategic decisions.
  • * We built research infrastructure and alignment at the same time, so the process and outcome moved together toward the same goal.
03

Research

Two tracks ran at once: internal interviews surfaced where the five departments disagreed, while external research focused on screens analytics flagged as failing.

We held 1:1 sessions with each department. Customer sessions were split into 30 minutes for Q&A on shopping and sustainability, and 30 minutes for usability testing. A handful of participants aged 24–64 took part.

The regimen guide drew over a million page views with a 46% bounce rate. This was included in the usability script and confirmed by a second signal: 94% of happiness-team contact was guidance customers could not find themselves.

What the research revealed

  • There was an influence gap. Teams shaping the brand had no exposure to customer service feedback. Reframe the project around alignment, not just digital experience.
  • Regimen pages failed the non-expert. Customers unfamiliar with active ingredients sometimes couldn't follow the reasoning behind Deciem's recommended combinations.
  • Sustainability interest outpaced infrastructure. Deciem ran zero-waste boxes in stores but had nothing online to market and support the cause.
Fig. 01 A card-sorting session run with cross-functional teams — each note represented a customer pain point, sorted live into a shared prioritization framework.
04

Process

  1. 01

    Interviewed all five departments to surface the real disagreement. Running the sessions separately was deliberate to ensure all perspectives were represented. In a seperate joint session, the team with the most authority set the terms and spoke the most.

  2. 02

    Built a prioritization framework and ran card sorting to force cross-team consensus. Turned each customer pain point into a card and had teams sort them live. When priorities differed — marketing wanted campaign visibility, customer service wanted product clarity — I ran the discussion until both sides could see how their priority mapped onto actual customer feedback. Influence came from participation, not from my authority.

  3. 03

    Used analytics to decide what to test, rather than guessing. Pulled drop-off data to identify the highest-friction screens and wrote them into the usability script, so limited qualitative time was spent only on proven problem areas.

  4. 04

    Tested three regimen-page variants with non-experts. Each version explained the recommendation a little differently. Some grouped options by skin type, others by ingredient function or by overall routine. The aim was to see which explanation made it easiest for people unfamiliar with active ingredients to understand why they’d received that recommendation.

  5. 05

    Prototyped two Spark AR concepts to solve product education where customers already were. Built virtual foundation matching with the customer happiness team, calibrated by the people who handled the products daily, and a Terracycle filter that extends the in-store zero-waste program online by identifying a bottle and routing it to the correct recycling stream with AR filters.

Fig. 02 The two Spark AR concepts — Virtual foundation matching, and a Terracycle filter that identifies a bottle and points it to the correct recycling stream.
05

Solution

The project resulted in a unified, cross-departmental understanding of both brand positioning and customer needs, leading to greater organizational alignment.

A clear roadmap was developed that distinguished immediate, actionable improvements from broader, long-term initiatives. Additionally, two augmented reality concepts were introduced to support education and sustainability efforts beyond traditional retail settings.

Why Spark AR. The platform choice was the strategy — Deciem's customers already used it, which removed the acquisition step a standalone app or a new site section would have required.

Design system elements. The regimen-page testing produced a component library that the internal team kept using after the engagement ended.

Fig. 03 The Ordinary regimen builder was prototyped on mobile first based on analytical data of browsing and viewing habits of customers.

What was delivered

  • A shared, cross-departmental understanding of positioning and customer need, replacing five competing versions.
  • A prioritization roadmap separating quick wins from larger scope-of-work.
  • Shipped a regimen builder live on theordinary.com
  • Two AR concepts addressing product education and sustainability beyond physical retail.
06

Impact and results

Five departments aligned on one prioritized view of customer need, and a two-tier roadmap gave leadership a sequencing decision instead of an undifferentiated backlog.

Business outcome. The personalized regimen builder shipped on theordinary.com as a direct result of the research and roadmap I delivered; I owned the prioritization and feature requirements that guided its development. Deciem's 2020 goals for the regimen guide — conversion up to 5%, with a reduction of 16% bounce rate. I can't attribute those metrics to this work alone, but the roadmap and research were key inputs as the team worked toward those targets.

Did the AR concepts ship? No. Both stayed at concept stage. Business priorities shifted toward core product improvements, and the technical work needed to meet Deciem's quality standards took longer than the engagement's timeline allowed.

What shipped, what didn't

  • The personalized regimen builder launched — the clearest outcome of the research.
  • Both Spark AR concepts remained concepts, deprioritized as the business shifted focus and resources to other initiatives.

What worked

Making the research undeniable rather than persuasive. Card sorting let teams build the priority list themselves, and unfiltered interview footage removed the room's ability to argue with the interpretation.

What I'd do differently

Negotiate a clear attribution model before starting rather than relying on company-wide analytics after the fact. Widen the sample beyond Toronto. For future projects, I would prioritize setting up attribution models in collaboration with stakeholders from day one to ensure impact is measurable and recognized across teams. I would also expand recruitment to include more geographically and demographically diverse participants, tailoring the sample to match the context and business goals. By embedding these lessons early in a new project, I can drive clearer alignment and demonstrate outcomes more effectively.

What I learned

The gap in most organizations isn't a lack of customer insight—it is that the insight sits with the teams that have the least power to act on it. Make this the central takeaway.
