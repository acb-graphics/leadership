CBS Sports was launching a new 24/7 live channel, all-access experience called CBS Sports HQ. We helped build a connected product experience across TV and mobile platforms in parallel with the broadcast team testing and launching the new channel.

01

Challenge

The product needed to be built within CBS Sports' existing architecture, featuring a live cinematic experience for the all-access app.

The current product's surfing experience had a high drop-off rate, as too much content led to cognitive overload for the end user. The challenge was to design an experience that surfaced live content viewing while still giving users the option to discover video-on-demand content.

Why it was hard

  • Had to build within CBS Sports' existing architecture, not a clean slate.
  • The current surfing experience had a high drop-off rate from cognitive overload.
  • Needed to balance a cinematic, live-first focus with easy discoverability of on-demand content.
Fig. 01 Reconstruction of the cross-platform device set the experience had to ship on simultaneously — TV, laptop, tablet and phone, all sharing one connected structure.
02

Strategy

The strategy was to create a model that mimicked surfing live TV at launch, while hinting at relevant content to customers.

The app also had to share a similar experience across all platforms. We started by mapping the basic structure of the user flow with wireframes, then met with each Tech Platform Lead to walk through blue-sky ideas and wireframes — which helped us scope features down based on the feasibility of each platform.

How platform scope was set

  • Wireframes mapped the base user flow before any visual design began.
  • Each Tech Platform Lead reviewed blue-sky ideas against real platform constraints.
  • Features were scaled down per platform based on feasibility, not redesigned from scratch.

03

Discovery

We looked at live TV news channels for inspiration.

We decided picture-in-picture would be the solution that combines the live experience with the discoverability of other content. In this phase, we stitched together every scenario of the connected TV experience — instances that needed to cover both a new and a continuing user.

Discovery focus

  • Live TV news formats as the reference point for combining "always on" with discoverability.
  • Picture-in-picture chosen as the mechanism to keep live content visible while browsing.
  • Scenarios covered both new-user and continuing-user paths through the connected experience.

04

Approach

After the launch of Apple TV's MVP, our team made adjustments during sprints based on feedback from early adopters.

That feedback helped refine the experience on Roku and Amazon Fire TV. We mocked up the designs in high fidelity and presented them to stakeholders. A few adjustments were made to the live-viewing experience — including changing the home page from a full-screen video to a collapsed video, giving users a better sense of control over what else they could navigate to.

What changed after launch

  • Apple TV's MVP shipped first; Roku and Fire TV were refined from real early-adopter feedback.
  • High-fidelity mockups were presented to stakeholders each sprint.
  • Home page shifted from full-screen video to a collapsed video, clarifying what else users could navigate to.
05

Outcome

The result was a connected CBS Sports HQ experience spanning TV and mobile — striking a balance between an always-on live broadcast and easy access to on-demand highlights.
