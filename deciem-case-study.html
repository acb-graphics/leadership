Deciem is an umbrella of beauty brands — The Ordinary, NIOD, Hylamide — founded on the principle of being transparent about ingredients and production costs, for affordable and inclusive skin care.

01

Challenge

Deciem deeply understood their internal strategy for product-market growth, but had no clarity on customer perspectives under each brand.

I started consulting as a Senior User Experience Designer and Researcher to understand and grow Deciem's brand and vision for customer engagement in 2019.

Why it was hard

  • Deciem knew their product strategy well but had no clear picture of customer perspective, per brand.
  • Internal teams — service, sales, marketing, design, branding — were each solving different problems, unaligned.
  • Needed to build both research infrastructure and internal consensus at the same time.

02

Strategy

I researched customer opinions of the umbrella brand and created a roadmap for innovative digital solutions that extended beyond physical retail engagement.

What the roadmap covered

  • Customer opinions gathered across the whole brand umbrella, not just one product line.
  • A roadmap for digital solutions extending beyond in-store engagement.
  • Set the direction for the discovery work that followed.
03

Discovery

I met individually with key members of customer service, floor sales, marketing, design and branding to strategize a problem worth prioritizing and solving.

Each team had its own agenda and was disconnected from the product roadmap. Teams like marketing, design and branding didn't understand the frustrations customer service heard directly, since they didn't speak with customers regularly.

In these meetings, I ran card-sorting tasks to find quick wins within a prioritization framework — surfacing what was urgent enough to impact Deciem's brand perception. It became clear these teams needed alignment on customer feedback, and that usability testing was needed on Deciem's site, since most complaints stemmed from the e-commerce experience. I also pulled Google Analytics drop-off data to identify the vital screens to bring into usability testing.

Discovery methods

  • 1:1 interviews across customer service, floor sales, marketing, design and branding.
  • Card-sorting sessions to prioritize quick wins against brand-perception impact.
  • Google Analytics drop-off data pulled directly into usability-testing scripts.
Fig. 02 Reconstruction of a card-sorting session run with cross-functional teams — each note represented a customer pain point, sorted live into a shared prioritization framework.
04

Approach

I invited customers aged 24–64 for qualitative research.

They were from Toronto, and each interview split into 30 minutes of Q&A on shopping behaviors and thoughts on sustainability — the brand's next development focus — and 30 minutes of usability testing on the web pages with the most issues.

This research gave internal teams a cohesive understanding of their positioning, goals, and customer needs beyond physical retail. The result was a clear list of quick wins for bug fixes, plus a separate list of larger, less urgent work that would require bigger teams to execute.

Research design

  • Participants aged 24–64, based in Toronto.
  • 30 min Q&A on shopping behavior and sustainability, paired with 30 min of usability testing on the highest-issue pages.
  • Output split into two buckets: quick-fix bugs, and larger scope-of-work items needing bigger teams.

Fig. 03 Reconstruction of the two Spark AR concepts — Virtual Foundation Matching, and a Terracycle filter that identifies a bottle and points it to the correct recycling stream.

In addition to the research, I explored new technology that could help with product education — another clear pain point for customers. I used Spark AR as the mobile platform, since most of Deciem's customers are on Instagram and Facebook, and developed two concepts. For Virtual Foundation Matching, I worked with the customer happiness team and lab assistants to calibrate colors against real skin tones. For the Terracycle filter, I worked with the sustainability team to conceptualize a camera that could identify parts of a product and label them for the correct recycling facility.

05

Outcome

The research gave Deciem's internal teams a cohesive understanding of their positioning, goals, and customer needs beyond physical retail — closing the gap between departments that had each been working from a different picture of the brand.
