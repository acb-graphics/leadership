Case Study — 0→1 Product Strategy, Research & Brand

Home

Turning an unvalidated founder idea into a tested product direction, brand, and roadmap in 8 weeks.

Client
Home (early-stage fintech — savings + advice for first-time homebuyers)
Role
Sole designer-researcher
Team
Renga
Duration
8 weeks, kickoff to final shareout

A founder came to us with conviction and a concept: a product that would combine savings and advice so first-time buyers could actually get to a down payment.

01

Situation

What they didn't have was evidence — no product, no validated market, no definition of what a first release should even contain.

The idea had momentum but nothing underneath it to hand to investors or engineers.

Why it had stalled

  • No validation. Market fit was assumed, not tested — and nothing could responsibly be built until that assumption was checked.
  • No MVP definition. There was a feature wishlist, but no shared view of what the minimum viable product actually was or which problem it solved first.
  • No room to be sequential. Strategy, research, brand, positioning, and concept testing all had to happen inside a single 8-week window, in parallel, with one designer-researcher.
02

Objective

Prove or disprove the concept quickly, and leave the founder with enough evidence and structure to raise money and hire a permanent team.

My role: Led. Sole designer-researcher on the engagement, reporting directly to the client-founder. I owned research design, discovery, concept testing, UX and prototyping end to end, and co-led brand and positioning workshops with the Renga team.

Timeframe: 8 weeks, kickoff to final stakeholder shareout.

What success looked like

  • A validated problem statement — grounded in primary research, not founder intuition.
  • A differentiated brand and positioning that could carry an investor conversation.
  • A tested prototype demonstrating the core experience end to end.
  • A product roadmap the client could execute against without us.
03

Actions

  1. 01

    Structured an 8-week plan that front-loaded evidence. Sequenced strategy, research, positioning, and concept testing so validation gated design work, rather than running design and research in parallel and hoping they'd agree.

  2. 02

    Facilitated four positioning workshops to force decisions. Ran four 1.5-hour sessions with the founder to lock mission, positioning, tone and voice — converting a fuzzy idea into language the business could actually be built and pitched on.

  3. 03

    Mapped the competitive landscape across the entire home-buying journey. Plotted competitors against the six stages from saving to selling, exposing that nearly every player owned a single stage — which became the strategic wedge and the anchor slide of the investor deck.

  4. 04

    Designed and fielded a 410-response study, then converted it into a qualitative pipeline. Ran a quantitative survey segmenting respondents by their stage in the buying journey, then recruited from within that pool for video interviews — so concept testing spoke to real buyers at the exact stage each concept targeted.

  5. 05

    Built and tested a prototype the founder could hand to a team. Designed the onboarding and savings-goal flow, validated it in interviews across each buying stage, and paired it with a roadmap that translated "what the founder wanted to build" into the problem worth solving first.

05

Reflection

What worked

Refusing to design before there was evidence. The founder arrived knowing what they wanted to build; the survey and interviews reframed the engagement around what problem was actually worth solving, and that reframe is what made the roadmap credible enough to hire against. Recruiting interview participants out of the survey pool was the highest-leverage decision of the project — it turned one research instrument into two and made concept testing sharper than a cold-recruited panel would have been.

What I'd refine

The survey drew on my own network, which is fast and free but skews the sample toward people like me. With more time or budget I'd have paired it with a screened panel to check whether the segment patterns held outside my circle. I'd also have instrumented the waitlist page more deliberately from day one — it generated a real demand signal, and I'd want that quantified rather than observed.

What I learned

In a 0→1 engagement, the deliverable isn't the prototype — it's the client's ability to continue without you. The measure of this project wasn't the screens; it was that the founder could write job descriptions, brief a team, and defend the direction in a room I wasn't in.
