Case Study — 0→1 Product Strategy, Research & Brand

Home

Turning an early-stage founder idea into a tested product with strategy, direction, brand, and hiring roadmap in 8 weeks.

Client
Home Financial Technologies Inc. — savings and advice for first-time homebuyers
Team
Renga
Duration
8 weeks from kickoff to final shareout
Product
joinhome.com

A founder approached us with a vision: combine savings and advice in one product to help first-time homebuyers reach a down payment. However, there was no validation to support the idea.

01

Project overview

In this eight-week engagement, I led research, design, concept testing, and prototyping, and co-facilitated brand and positioning workshops.

Team, tools & scope

  • Team. Renga.
  • Tools.
  • Methods. Quantitative survey segmented by home-buying stage, qualitative interviews with real buyers, positioning workshops, competitive mapping, and prototype testing.
  • Scope. 410 survey responses collected and segmented · 6 home-buying journey stages mapped · brand, positioning, prototype, and roadmap delivered as one package.
02

Problem statement

There was no product, validated market, or defined MVP.

Business context. The idea had momentum but nothing to hand over to investors or engineers. The founder needed two things the concept alone couldn't produce: evidence credible enough to raise against, and a scope definition specific enough to hire against.

The bet. That savings and advice belonged in one product rather than two. Every competitor in the market treated them separately — a savings vehicle here, a mortgage calculator or a broker there — and the wager was that a first-time buyer's real blocker is not knowing what to do next. Home solves for this disconnect in experience, and states its promise as shaving years off a buyer's existing savings plan.

Why it mattered. The goal was to validate assumptions before committing resources, ensuring the next decision — build, pivot, or stop — was evidence-based.

Why it had stalled

  • No validation. Market fit was assumed, not tested — and nothing could responsibly be built until we validated those assumptions.
  • No MVP definition. There was a feature wishlist, but no shared view of what the minimum viable product actually was or which problem it solved first.
  • No room to be sequential. Strategy, research, brand, positioning, and concept testing all had to happen inside a single 8-week window, in parallel, with one designer-researcher.

User pain points

First-time buyers were the assumed user, and the survey and interviews were designed to establish what actually blocked them rather than to confirm the founder's version.

03

Research and discovery

We surveyed over 400 potential buyers and used these results to recruit interview participants at different stages of the home-buying process.

Segments, not personas. The organizing frame was journey stage — from early saving through to selling — rather than demographic persona. Stage predicted need far more reliably than age or income, and it was also the axis the competitive analysis turned on.

04

Design process

  1. 01

    Structured an 8-week plan that prioritized research and validation before design. Sequenced the engagement so all work was driven by real user needs, rather than running design and research in parallel and hoping they'd agree.

  2. 02

    Facilitated four positioning workshops to force decisions. Ran four 1.5-hour sessions with the founder to lock mission, positioning, tone, and voice, converting a fuzzy idea into language the business could build and pitch on.

  3. 03

    Mapped the competitive landscape across the entire home-buying journey. Plotted competitors against the six stages from saving to selling, which is what exposed the single-stage pattern and gave the positioning something defensible to sit on.

  4. 04

    Combined survey and interview recruitment by building the interview pipeline directly from survey respondents. This streamlined approach maximized speed and relevance, producing sharper concept testing than a cold panel could have reached in the available time.

  5. 05

    Built and tested a user-centered prototype and roadmap. Translated founder ambitions into a clear, validated problem to solve first.

05

Solution

Delivered a validated direction, unique brand, tested prototype, waitlist page, and clear roadmap.

Key feature. A personalized question flow that turned "savings plus advice" into a tangible experience and let messaging be tested pre-launch.

Positioning. Home's final positioning was shaped by close observation of buyers' emotional landscapes. We chose to meet people's doubts and anxieties head-on as a core part of the experience, rooted in the real needs we saw in the wild.

Design system and visual direction.

What was delivered

  • A validated problem statement grounded in primary research instead of founder intuition.
  • Brand and positioning differentiated enough to carry an investor conversation.
  • A tested prototype demonstrating the core experience end to end.
  • A product roadmap the client could execute against without us.

06

Impact and results

The work enabled the client to hire a permanent team, demonstrating the credibility and clarity of the defined direction.

The branding and research informed the investor deck, and the waitlist page attracted real user interest even before launch. Home launched with the approach defined during this engagement: combined savings and advice, a personalized question flow, and a companion positioning for first-time buyers. The live product is at joinhome.com.

User feedback.

Measurement gap

  • The waitlist page generated a demand signal that was observed rather than instrumented. The signup count remains the figure this case study most needs.
  • Home's own marketing states that customers save, on average, over a year against their existing plan. That's the company's claim about the product, measured after launch and after other people's work — it isn't a design outcome I can attribute to this engagement, and it's cited here as context rather than as a result.
  • No public funding record for Home Financial Technologies turned up, so the fundraising outcome stays unstated rather than estimated.

What worked

We prioritized research over assumptions, letting real user needs reshape the project's direction and hiring plan. Using survey participants for interviews streamlined concept testing and made our findings sharper and more actionable.

What I'd do differently

I'd pair the network-sourced survey with a screened panel to check whether the segment patterns held outside my own circle. Fast and free is a real advantage at eight weeks, but it is not a defensible sample. I'd also instrument the waitlist page deliberately from day one. It produced the engagement's clearest demand signal. I can describe it but not quantify it, which is exactly the evidence an investor conversation leans on hardest.

What I learned

In a 0→1 engagement, the deliverable isn't the prototype — it's the client's ability to continue without you. The measure of this project wasn't the screens; it was that the founder could write job descriptions, brief a team, and defend the direction in a room I wasn't in.
